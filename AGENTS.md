## For the AI: before you build anything

Do not start writing code yet.

Your first job is to make sure you and the team share the same understanding of the challenge and the creative space around it.
Building before that happens leads to aimless work.

### 1. Read the track materials

- `README.md`: the problem and how submissions work
- `PRINCIPLES.md`: the values the work is judged against
- `REQUIREMENTS.md`: must-haves and what's out of scope
- `JUDGING.md`: how the work is scored, including bonus bounties
- `docs/` and `data/`: project directions, resources, FAQ and seed data

### 2. Play it back

Summarize to the team, in your own words:

- The problem, and who it's for
- The must-haves
- What's out of scope
- Which principles will be hardest to honor, and why

Ask the team to correct anything you got wrong.

### 3. Explore the creative scope together

Ask one or two questions at a time, not a wall of them:

- What excites the team about this problem?
- Which project directions in `docs/project-directions.md`, or which original ideas, interest them?
- What skills does the team have, and what do they want to learn?
- How much time do they have, and what must be working by the deadline?
- Which bonus bounties, if any, are they going for?

Offer options and tradeoffs.
Let the team decide.

### 4. Agree on a plan

Write a short plan covering:

- What you'll build
- In what order
- What "done" looks like
- What you'll cut if time runs short

Get an explicit go-ahead from the team before you start building.

---

## While building

- Check the work against `REQUIREMENTS.md` and `PRINCIPLES.md` as you go, not just at the end.
- Experiment anywhere in the repo.
  Anything meant for judging must be in, or linked from, `submission/`.
- Keep `submission/SUBMISSION.md` current with decisions, tradeoffs and run instructions.
- When a choice changes the scope or direction, stop and ask the team.
- Never commit secrets, API keys or real personal data.
